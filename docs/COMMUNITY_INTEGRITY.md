# Community and Social Integrity

This document governs Feed, Discuss, Events, Network, Stories, reactions, follows, RSVPs, and other community features.

## Principle

Civil Rights Hub must not manufacture the appearance of a community.

A small genuine community is acceptable. Fake activity is not.

## Content classes

### Genuine user content

Posts, comments, stories, follows, reactions, messages, forum contributions, profiles, and RSVPs created by authenticated users through normal product flows.

These may be shown as user-generated content subject to moderation/privacy/RLS.

### Official/editorial content

Educational or editorial material published by a clearly identified Civil Rights Hub/We The People News/system account.

It must not imitate a member, invent a first-person experience, use a synthetic persona, or carry fabricated engagement counters.

Factual claims still require appropriate sourcing.

### Legacy seeded content

Old content generated by migration/seed scripts, anonymous usernames, bulk insertions, or unknown historical tooling.

Default treatment: contaminated until proven otherwise.

## Prohibited activity

Never seed or generate:

- fake user accounts/usernames;
- fake first-person posts;
- fake forum personalities/discussions;
- fake replies/comments;
- fake follows/network relationships;
- fake likes/reactions/shares/views/bookmarks;
- fake poll votes;
- fake story views;
- fake event RSVPs/attendance;
- fake popularity/trending counters;
- content attached to a real user's account without that user's action/approval;
- event listings without a durable source.

Do not use generated content to make screenshots/demos look active in the production database.

## Current recovery standard

Before restoring a held community surface, verify production—not just schema files.

### Feed

Require:

- `posts` and dependent tables exist;
- RLS is enabled and policies match intended public/private behavior;
- browser grants do not bypass intended policy boundaries;
- known seeded content is removed/quarantined;
- remaining public rows have a defensible origin;
- media Storage bucket and object policies support the actual client bucket name;
- posting/comment/reaction/bookmark/share/report flows are tested with an authenticated user.

### Discuss

Require:

- forum thread/post tables and RLS are correct;
- legacy bulk/generated threads are quarantined or clearly excluded;
- new threads are attributable to real authenticated users or an explicit official/system identity;
- view/post/like counters are derived from real activity rather than seed values.

### Events

Require:

- public events have a real account organizer or verified authoritative source path;
- dates/location/virtual links/registration info are source-backed and current;
- old unproven seed events are withheld;
- RSVP counts are derived from real rows.

### Network

Require:

- user profiles represent actual accounts;
- follower/following counts derive from real relationship rows;
- no suggested-profile or online-count generator invents activity;
- privacy settings are respected.

### Stories

Require:

- story rows are account-backed;
- views are real rows/derived counts;
- expiration is enforced;
- Storage/media paths are owner-scoped when media is supported.

## Storage contract

The current SocialFeed client uses the `posts` Supabase Storage bucket.

The production contract must include:

- bucket `posts` exists;
- public read only if intended;
- authenticated upload policy restricted to a user-owned prefix such as `<auth.uid()>/...`;
- authenticated delete/update policy restricted to the owner where needed;
- MIME types and file-size limits aligned with what the UI accepts.

Do not document or audit a different bucket name than the client actually uses.

## Legacy corpus detection

Signals of synthetic/seeded data include:

- hundreds of rows created on one migration day;
- many rows with NULL account owners;
- large sets of generated usernames/personas;
- static high view/like/comment counts despite few/no real accounts;
- event records with no organizer account/source;
- generic first-person stories created in bulk;
- records matching historical seed migrations.

These signals justify quarantine/review; they should not automatically be presented as genuine activity.

## Quarantine policy

When removing a legacy social corpus:

1. snapshot rows with source table/id and reason;
2. preserve audit metadata privately;
3. remove or exclude the contaminated public rows;
4. reset derived/trending counters that were influenced by seed data;
5. rebuild counts from genuine rows;
6. verify public surfaces against the clean state.

## Counts and analytics

Display only values derived from real activity or clearly identified analytical sources.

Never set starter floors such as “200 likes,” “50 people online,” or fabricated views to make the platform appear busy.

If there is no activity, show zero or omit the metric.

## Moderation

User-generated content can be false, disputed, or unlawful without becoming a platform-verified claim.

Moderation labels should distinguish user reports/allegations from adjudicated facts. Automated moderation may assist triage but should not silently convert allegations into verified findings.

## External sharing

Social share buttons should share the canonical Civil Rights Hub URL and current Open Graph/Twitter metadata. Branding/favicon/social-card assets must exist at the advertised URLs so messaging platforms do not fall back to stale vendor assets.

## Acceptance checklist

Before declaring social/community “back up”:

- [ ] production audit passes;
- [ ] synthetic legacy corpus handled;
- [ ] `posts` Storage bucket policies verified;
- [ ] authenticated post creation works;
- [ ] media upload works if enabled;
- [ ] comments/reactions/follows/RSVPs tested;
- [ ] public vs private visibility tested;
- [ ] zero fake starter engagement remains;
- [ ] frontend build/tests pass;
- [ ] production deployment verified;
- [ ] observability captures errors without sensitive content.
